\section{Related Work}

Shortest-path studies differ in what a path cost represents. A length-based
search minimizes travel distance on the graph. A kinematic search can instead
minimize travel time by accounting for heading, speed, and acceleration.
Congestion-weighted search estimates the desirability of a route under traffic.
Time-indexed planning represents when a robot can traverse a segment or occupy
a vertex. These formulations answer different questions even when they use
the same roadmap.

Seifert et al.\ \cite{seifert1995} &
Runs a separate discrete-event subsimulation for each promising route.
Selects the route with the shortest simulated travel time. &
Starts each run from the current AGV system state and simulates
until the requesting AGV reaches its destination. &
Uses simulation lookahead directly in the routing decision.
A travel-time lower bound can eliminate further trials. \\ \addlinespace
Klaas et al.\ \cite{klaas2011} &
Computes incremental shortest paths and selects a rerouting rule
when a conflict is predicted. &
Offline discrete-event lookahead evaluates alternative rules
and supplies training examples for a knowledge base. &
Uses the learned knowledge base online to account for longer-term
effects of local routing decisions. \\ \addlinespace
Ma et al.\ \cite{ma2019} &
Priority-Based Search branches on partial priority orders. Individual routes
are planned subject to the paths of higher-priority agents. &
Conflicts in planned trajectories determine which priority constraints are added. &
Shows how to search over priority orders in discrete MAPF. The search has
limitations on completeness and optimality. \\ \addlinespace
Andreychuk et al.\ \cite{andreychuk2019} &
Continuous-time Conflict-Based Search replans individual minimum-duration
paths with Safe Interval Path Planning (SIPP). &
Unsafe intervals derived from continuous-time conflicts constrain actions. &
Optimizes the sum of action durations under its motion model. The experiments
use constant-speed translation and omit turn dynamics. \\ \addlinespace
Li et al.\ \cite{li2021} &
Rolling-Horizon Collision Resolution repeatedly solves a windowed MAPF problem. &
Conflict resolution is limited to a finite planning horizon as new goals arrive. &
Demonstrates scalable lifelong planning in warehouses. System performance is
evaluated by throughput. \\ \addlinespace
Ali and Yakovlev \cite{ali2021} &
Prioritized SIPP searches with discretized motion states that include
heading and speed. &
Acceleration and deceleration constrain arrival times. Safe intervals
represent the trajectories of higher-priority robots. &
Demonstrates planning with kinematic constraints. Energy is outside the
route objective. \\ \addlinespace
Lou et al.\ \cite{lou2023} &
Dijkstra generates initial routes and Yen's algorithm generates alternatives
for conflict resolution. &
Predicted node visit times support conflict checks in a digital-twin system. &
Combines AGV task scheduling and alternative routing. Provides a precedent
for using a digital twin in route selection. \\ \addlinespace
Chen et al.\ \cite{chen2024} &
Graph search creates guide paths with costs for opposing flows and
congestion. A coordination policy executes movements. &
Flow counts from guide paths serve as congestion indicators. &
Improves lifelong MAPF throughput. Its congestion costs do not directly
produce arrival times. \\ \addlinespace
Zang et al.\ \cite{zang2025} &
A learned model updates guidance-graph weights. The planner uses these
weights to guide movement and route search. &
Recent traffic or guide-path usage informs the weight updates. &
Uses learning to adapt routing costs and improve throughput. The model
outputs guidance weights rather than robot arrival times. \\ \addlinespace
Combrink et al.\ \cite{combrink2025} &
Combines continuous-time search with asynchronous prioritized planning
for lifelong operation. &
Plans timed trajectories for volumetric agents under a planning budget. &
Extends continuous-time coordination to repeated tasks and evaluates
throughput. Energy is outside the route objective. \\ \addlinespace
Meissner and Massalski \cite{meissner2020} &
Develops and experimentally evaluates a model of AGV energy consumption. &
Vehicle operating inputs determine the energy estimate. &
Provides a basis for energy evaluation of candidate routes. The study
does not solve the multi-robot routing problem. \\ \addlinespace
Thalaivar et al.\ \cite{thalaivar2026} &
Compares A*, particle swarm optimization, and gravitational search in an
AGV scheduling framework. &
Uses a unit-speed travel-time proxy. Inter-AGV collision avoidance is
outside the planner. &
Compares makespan, energy, and distance. Also studies multi-objective
task assignment with NSGA-II. \\

\paragraph{Implications for route generation.}
For initial dispatch without a rack and for dispatch immediately after rack
drop-off, a free-flow planner can use an unloaded motion model to score
straight runs and heading changes. The predicted travel time should preserve
speed across consecutive collinear arcs when motion is uninterrupted.
Residual swept paths can inform the generation of candidates with less
potential interference. A digital-twin lookahead can then evaluate each set of
candidate routes from the same fleet state and under the same traffic-control
policy. The resulting makespan and fleet energy should be reported separately.
This comparison distinguishes the single-robot free-flow travel time used
during search from the fleet outcomes that emerge during execution.
